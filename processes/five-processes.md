# Five procedures to rehearse and adapt

These are fictional, unsigned drafts. Select the five processes that matter in your own company. Replace role names, evidence references and decision authorities. Give each procedure its own approval record before publication. Where a contract, legal hold or professional duty governs a decision, obtain the appropriate owner's interpretation.

## 1. Accept a new client

**Accountable owner:** Client acceptance lead. **Starts when:** A prospective client requests work. **Inputs:** Proposed service, client identity details held privately, applicable acceptance rules and current conflict register.

1. The intake coordinator opens a restricted matter record and assigns a reference. Collect only information needed to make the acceptance decision using the approved intake route.
2. The coordinator checks the client and related parties against the current conflict process. Record sources, date and match status. Ask for clarification if identity or scope is uncertain.
3. The acceptance lead reviews the risk, any match and any required specialist decision. A possible conflict or missing evidence places the matter on hold. Only the designated authority can record a clearance or permitted exception.
4. An authorised reviewer checks the supporting record and signs the decision. The coordinator updates the matter to accepted, declined or held and notifies the engagement lead through the internal channel. Start work only on recorded acceptance and any required signed terms.

**Resolve a conflict between copies:** An old intake sheet says “start on referral.” The owner checks current acceptance rules, records the decision and withdraws any conflicting instruction. **Walkthrough:** A clear case advances after approval. A name match remains held even when the referrer asks for immediate work.

## 2. Agree scope and approve a change

**Accountable owner:** Engagement lead. **Starts when:** A client requests new work or changes an agreed deliverable. **Inputs:** Accepted matter ID, signed terms, current scope, request and authority limits.

1. Record the requested change, deliverable, timing, fee implications and relevant data or AI restrictions against the matter. Keep the previous agreement accessible as a historical record.
2. Compare the request with the signed scope. If it is already covered, document the clause and confirm the delivery owner. If the boundary is unclear, ask the partner or contracts owner before committing.
3. For a material change, prepare a revision from the approved template. A second authorised reviewer checks the differences, client restrictions and approval limit. Obtain the required internal and client authorisations.
4. Record the exact agreed version, signatories, effective date and decision. Update the delivery brief with its controlled link. The team begins changed work once the relevant approvals are recorded.

**Resolve a conflict between copies:** A local template accepts verbal agreement for a fee change. The lead checks the signed terms and records which written authority applies. **Walkthrough:** An in-scope clarification records the clause. An unsigned expansion remains on hold.

## 3. Review and release AI-assisted client work

**Accountable owner:** Engagement partner or designated release owner. **Starts when:** A client-facing draft contains material AI assistance. **Inputs:** Draft version, engagement restrictions, current authoritative sources, calculation evidence and reviewer assignment.

1. The author identifies the tool and permitted task in the internal work record. Check that the data, account and use are permitted for this engagement. Escalate an unauthorised use through the private route.
2. The author opens each material source, checks its version and effective date and reconciles material facts, quotations and calculations. Record source IDs and place unsupported claims on hold.
3. A competent person other than the author checks the exact draft against those sources. Record required corrections and rerun checks on the revised draft. Obtain specialist or partner sign-off where the subject requires it.
4. The release owner checks the signed version against the file ready for dispatch. Record the review and dispatch references and times. A material edit after approval returns to step 3. Escalate a previously sent error to the engagement lead.

**Resolve a conflict between copies:** A legacy covering note permits author sign-off. The owner checks the applicable review rule and records who may release this version. **Walkthrough:** A reviewed draft goes out with matching version IDs. A changed figure or superseded source blocks dispatch until rechecked.

## 4. Grant, change and remove staff or contractor access

**Accountable owner:** People operations lead with the relevant information owner. **Starts when:** HR confirms a joiner, role change, contract expiry or departure. **Inputs:** Restricted staff reference, verified event and effective time, role, system owner and access matrix.

1. HR verifies the event and opens a restricted IT request. The manager identifies work duties, data groups, start/end times and any deputy requirement. Record who authorised the request.
2. The information owner approves the minimum required access and any time-limited exception. For a move, identify access to remove as well as access to add. Missing approvals leave the request pending.
3. Only the IT lead or their company-authorised delegate implements approved changes through the firm's standard process. Preserve required business records and follow the company's leaver timetable. Escalate a missed removal immediately.
4. IT records completion and role-based tests. The manager confirms the intended work can proceed and IT checks the former role can no longer reach the restricted material. The owner closes the request after both results, or records an exception and due date.

**Resolve a conflict between copies:** An old starter list grants the entire shared drive. The information owner compares this with the current role matrix and signs the intended minimum. **Walkthrough:** A joiner receives an authorised group. A mover retains the new role and loses old client access on schedule.

## 5. Close a matter and retire old working copies

**Accountable owner:** Records lead with the engagement owner. **Starts when:** Work finishes or a procedure is replaced. **Inputs:** Matter index, final deliverables, approval and dispatch records, retention schedule, hold status and list of working copies.

1. The engagement owner identifies the signed final deliverables and the evidence supporting release. Record the approved copy and its location in the matter index. Resolve missing approvals before closure.
2. The records lead checks the applicable retention rule, contract terms and any hold. Record the basis, responsible role and next review date. Route unclear or conflicting obligations to the authorised specialist.
3. Mark superseded working instructions clearly and point active work to the current controlled source. Ask IT to implement any approved archive or access change. Preserve material subject to retention or hold and obtain separate authorisation for disposal.
4. A second person opens the active link and locates the retained historical record with an authorised role. Record the results, exceptions and follow-up. Confirm closure with the records lead.

**Resolve a conflict between copies:** A tidy-up checklist says “delete drafts on closure” while a matter is under hold. The owner records the hold decision and preserves affected records. **Walkthrough:** A normal matter closes with a working current link. A held record remains preserved and disposal is blocked.
