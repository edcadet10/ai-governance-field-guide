# AI use and client work policy

**Draft for company approval.** Organisation [name] · Policy owner [role] · Approver [person/role] · Effective [date] · Version [number] · Review [date] · Approved tools list [internal location] · Private help/incident route [contact].

## Choose the right account

Use only a tool **and account type** approved for the task and information involved. Check the approved list for permitted data, purposes, owner and conditions. Use an individual company-managed account for authorised business work. Personal or unapproved accounts may not be used for company work under this draft. If you already used one, stop that use and contact the policy owner privately for help moving to an approved route.

## Decide what information may go in

Public material may be used in a listed tool for a permitted purpose. Internal information requires the approved company workspace and matching data classification. Client-confidential, HR, personal and other restricted information requires explicit authorisation for that data, purpose, tool and contract. Never enter passwords, access tokens or recovery codes. Use the smallest necessary excerpt. If any condition is unclear, pause and ask the information owner. Follow stricter client instructions.

## Check work before it leaves the firm

1. **Author.** Identify material AI assistance in the internal work record. Open the current approved sources and capture version, effective date and reference. Check calculations independently. Check every material factual claim and citation against the source.
2. **Competent reviewer.** For client-facing AI-assisted work, a named person other than the author reviews the actual version to be sent. For advice, contractual statements, financial calculations, HR or a material client decision, the responsible partner or authorised specialist signs as reviewer or adds the required approval. An AI tool cannot approve release.
3. **Release owner.** Record reviewer, outcome, date, approved version and evidence reference. Send only the approved version through the normal client channel. Recheck material changes after approval. Follow applicable engagement terms on AI use and disclosure.
4. **Hold or correct.** If a source is missing, outdated, conflicting or restricted, mark the draft on hold. Ask the source owner to settle it, correct the draft and obtain a fresh review. If an incorrect version was sent, notify the engagement lead promptly and follow the correction/incident process.

For internal, low-risk brainstorming the author checks accuracy before relying on the output. Decisions about people or regulated advice remain with the authorised human decision maker under the firm's existing professional and review procedures.

## Record exceptions and concerns

Request an exception before use. State the business need, data class, tool and account, intended recipients, alternative, safeguards and expiry. The policy owner obtains information-owner and privacy/security input as appropriate. Record the decision and restrict it to its approved scope. Escalate suspected exposure promptly through the private incident route. Record time, service and categories involved without redistributing content. The privacy or incident lead assesses notifications under applicable duties.

The policy owner reviews the register, sampled release records and overdue exceptions monthly at launch and whenever a tool, contract or material use changes. The sponsor approves updates, publishes the current copy, communicates the change and assigns a review date. Staff receive the short briefing and can ask questions privately.
