# Turn conflicting copies into one reliable procedure

1. **Choose five processes.** Ask the sponsor where a stale instruction could change a client or HR decision. Select five, identify one accountable owner for each and name a colleague who actually performs the job. The five supplied examples are starting points to replace or adapt.
2. **Find competing sources.** For each process, list the controlled library, shared drive, duplicate files, templates, Teams links and instructions people rely on from memory. Record source IDs, displayed dates and owners privately. A filename containing “final” or a newer modification date does not establish authority.
3. **Walk one case.** Ask a practitioner to show the last completed example with sensitive details removed. Capture the trigger, inputs, exact sequence, checks, exception route and proof of completion. Ask a second role where the written steps diverge from practice.
4. **Settle each contradiction.** Write the two instructions side by side. The owner checks the governing contract, current policy or authoritative source, asks the right specialist where needed, records the decision and effective date, and marks disputed items on hold. Keep a decision reference.
5. **Publish one controlled source.** The owner approves a version, location, effective date, audience and review date. Link from the normal workflow to that location. Mark other copies as superseded and record where they point. Arrange archiving, retention and any legal hold with the records owner; preserve records required for an older matter.
6. **Prove someone can follow it.** A colleague runs one ordinary and one exception case using the approved procedure. Record errors, corrections and approval of the final version. Repeat after a material change.
7. **Recheck the assistant.** Under an authorised test account, ask an ordinary work question. Inspect the cited document, location, version, date and text. Test directly in the repository as well. If Copilot still cites an old copy, record the prompt category and source IDs privately, use the approved source manually and ask IT to investigate indexing, sharing or duplicate locations. A correct answer on one prompt proves only that sample.

## A worked disagreement

Two copies of a release procedure disagree on whether the author may sign their own client letter. The process owner checks the agreed engagement review rule and selects the independent-review path. The approved procedure identifies the reviewer, version and dispatch evidence. The old file points to the current copy, while historical client records follow retention rules. A second person then tries to send a materially changed draft on the old approval; the process routes it back for review.

## What to write for each process

Use a short title, accountable owner, trigger, required inputs, numbered instructions, decision points, stop/escalation route, output, evidence, source location, version, effective date and next review. The owner signs the exact version and the independent walkthrough result. Store real client/HR examples in the approved private location.
