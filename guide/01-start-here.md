# Bring AI use and company documents under control

**Decision for the sponsor.** Authorise one review led by a business owner. Name a person who can approve the AI policy, one owner for each of five important processes, a privacy contact, and an IT lead who alone carries out agreed access changes. The immediate business outcome is a client deliverable the firm can stand behind and a current source its staff can find.

## Begin with a 30-minute meeting

Bring one redacted example of an AI-assisted output, two conflicting copies of a procedure, one suspected over-shared folder, and the question a client or insurer asked. Ask who could be harmed, which decision is due next and what evidence leadership can already produce. If the concern involves a live disclosure of personal or client information, notify the firm's incident owner privately straight away. Record the reference number and keep the sensitive evidence in the approved system.

## Follow these steps in order

| Step | Who leads | What to do in plain English | Finish when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Find actual use | Business lead and privacy contact | Tell staff why you are asking. Send the short survey. Check answers against the approved tool list, invoices and IT-provided counts. Interview people in overlooked roles. | You can describe tool, account type, job, information used, owner and gaps. Keep restricted name-level follow-up separate from the leadership summary. |
| 2. Set the release rule | Managing partner or sponsor | Choose permitted uses, name who reviews client-facing work, and decide who can approve exceptions. Try the draft policy on a past redacted report. | A named reviewer can accept, correct or withhold the report and show what was checked. |
| 3. Reconcile five processes | Five process owners | Find each version, observe someone doing the job and compare disputed steps against actual practice and governing requirements. Approve one current copy and mark the rest with their disposition. | Each process has an owner, a current version, a controlled location, a review date and an independent walkthrough. |
| 4. Give IT the access list | Information owners and IT lead | List the affected folder or site, current audience, intended audience, business impact, exception and recommended change. The business owner approves. IT alone executes and tests. | Each item has a priority, accountable owner, evidence reference, IT ticket and verification or an explicit pending reason. |
| 5. Test a real decision | Engagement lead | Use an approved example. Check which source Copilot cites. Cross-check a material claim. A designated reviewer signs before client release. | A clean source reference, reviewed output and release decision can be located together. |
| 6. Answer for the controls | Sponsor | Assemble a short, redacted evidence pack for the insurer or client. State what was observed, what is still open and when it will be reviewed. | The sponsor can support each external claim with dated operating evidence and an owner. |

## Where to put effort first

An HR or client folder visible to an unintended audience goes to IT and its business owner immediately. A wrong template used in a client pack comes next, with a temporary instruction to open the known approved source directly. Stale general training materials can wait behind these. If the data needed to identify the correct document is missing, pause that use case and ask the owner to settle the decision before using the output externally.

For a 70-person firm, an illustrative sequence is an initial week of discovery and triage, a second week of policy and process reconciliation, and a third week of owner walkthroughs, IT handover and assurance. Owners' availability determines the schedule. A small firm can combine roles. Keep a second competent checker for material client work; agree a suitable independent reviewer where one person would otherwise both draft and release.

## The decision to extend to another team

Agree targets at the start. Track who was reached and which teams remain unobserved, how many of the five processes have an approved source and a successful walkthrough, the number of high-priority access findings independently retested, and the reviewed releases in a defined sample. Report counts and denominators. A low survey response leaves a discovery gap. An untested permission change leaves exposure unresolved. A signed policy alone establishes the rule; a reviewed work sample shows whether it operated.

At the first monthly review the sponsor chooses one of three actions. Expand to another team when the sources, access checks and review records support the decision. Revise a control when it repeatedly breaks down. Suspend a particular AI use when the owner cannot protect the information or validate the result. Record the owner and next review date for every open item.

## Adapt this to your company

Replace partner, manager and IT lead with your real roles. Replace SharePoint or Copilot with the document repository and assistant your team actually uses. Insert your professional duties, client-contract restrictions, retention schedule, country-specific privacy requirements and incident contacts before approval. The UK example uses ICO guidance when reviewing staff activity.
