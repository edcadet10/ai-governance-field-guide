# Find the tools people actually use

## Send a short, transparent request

Suggested introduction for the sponsor: “We are mapping how AI helps people do their jobs and where we need better guidance. Please include personal-account and occasional work use. Describe the task and the type of information. Keep client names, HR details and prompts out of the survey. Only [named roles] will see the restricted responses. We will retain them until [date] and report patterns to leadership. Contact [private route] for help or to report possible disclosure.”

Choose an identified survey with a clear purpose when named follow-up is necessary, or use confidential team responses and a separate opt-in follow-up route. Tell respondents which approach applies. The privacy lead should assess necessity, proportionality, transparency and local requirements before using staff activity data. Prefer existing business records and owner-provided summaries. Do not inspect personal accounts, private messages, prompt histories or employee devices for this exercise. [ICO worker monitoring guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/employment/monitoring-workers/data-protection-and-monitoring-workers/)

## Ask these questions

1. Which team or role do you work in? Which AI tools or built-in assistants have you used **for work** during the agreed review period? Include “none” and “unsure.”
2. For each tool, was it a company-managed account, a personal account or an account you cannot identify?
3. Which task did it help with? How often? Who receives the result?
4. Which categories of information were involved: public, internal, client-confidential, HR/personal, or unknown? Select categories only.
5. Who checks the output, against what reference, and who is allowed to send it to a client?
6. How do you find the current source? Where do duplicate documents or access problems slow you down?
7. If you need private follow-up, how should the restricted reviewer contact you? Use the normal private incident channel for an urgent concern.

## Build the inventory and close gaps

Enter one row per tool, account type and meaningful use. Use a restricted staff reference when necessary; give leadership aggregated results by team. Distinguish staff-reported use, approved licenses, IT-observed activity and unknowns. A license proves entitlement, not actual use. One person's use of two tools counts as one respondent and two use records. Preserve these denominators. Never infer that personal use is absent from a corporate usage report.

Ask the IT lead for permitted configuration or usage summaries, finance for subscription evidence, and a team lead for a redacted workflow walkthrough. Reconcile discrepancies and preserve the source and date. Prioritise personal-account use involving client/HR information, client outputs with no named reviewer, and material decisions grounded in old sources. Give each a business owner, interim action and decision date.

For example, if 52 of 70 invited staff respond, record **52 respondents and 18 unknown**. That result cannot support a statement about all 70. Sample remaining roles through their manager, update the coverage register and record the point at which the sponsor accepts the residual uncertainty. These are illustrative numbers.
