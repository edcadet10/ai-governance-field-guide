# Try the method in a 20-minute meeting

Everything on this page is fictional. Bring an author, a reviewer and an IT or records representative. Use the questions first and then compare decisions.

## Give the team this case

A 70-person firm invited everyone to its survey. Fifty-two replied. One person reports using a personal ChatGPT account to rephrase public marketing copy. Another person uses company Copilot to draft a client report. The first use has no approved account. The second user's current source cites a five-day review period; Copilot links to a superseded ten-day instruction. The reviewer signed draft 2 and the author subsequently changed the published figure in draft 3. Separately, the HR owner shows IT a broad inherited staff group on an HR library. Nobody has established whether an unauthorised person opened a document.

## Ask for four decisions

1. How many people's use is still unknown and what should leadership hear?
2. Which source governs the client report? Who can authorise draft 3?
3. What must the IT lead receive now? What evidence would close that finding?
4. What can the firm tell an insurer today?

## Compare your decisions

1. Record 52 responses and 18 outstanding. Record the personal-account use separately from company-managed use, follow up privately and move the work to an approved route. Keep personal responses out of the sponsor's summary.
2. The process owner confirms the approved effective version and reconciles five versus ten days. Put draft 3 on hold, verify the figure and affected narrative, and obtain the competent reviewer's sign-off for draft 3 before dispatch. Record sources, decision and times.
3. Open a P1 handoff with HR as information owner and IT as change owner. Record the observed inheritance and intended group privately. Invoke the firm's incident assessment route. Close the access issue only after IT documents allowed and denied direct access and the owner validates business continuity. Record a separate Copilot result.
4. State the survey coverage and what the example actually demonstrated. An open access item and a withheld draft cannot support a claim that all folders or client releases passed. Set a follow-up date and use a real sample before making operating claims.
